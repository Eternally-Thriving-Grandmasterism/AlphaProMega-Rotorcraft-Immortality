# WP-04 — Daedalus-Skin Rotorcraft Requirements & Coupon Matrix

**Status:** ACTIVE  
**Opened:** 2026-08-13 by Permanent PATSAGi Councils under TOLC 8  
**Depends on:** WP-01 Rank 4 & Rank 12 (blade separation / fatigue)  
**Target:** Define performance requirements and the initial coupon / subscale test matrix so materials maturation can proceed in parallel with architecture work.

---

## Design Imperative

Main rotor blades and critical load-path structures are high-cycle fatigue items. Traditional damage-tolerant design relies on inspection intervals and retirement lives. Daedalus-Skin lineage seeks to add autonomous or semi-autonomous healing capability so that small cracks and limited impact/erosion damage can be recovered without immediate grounding or major repair.

---

## Core Requirements

**DS-REQ-001**  
Self-healing capability shall be applicable to main rotor blade spars and/or skins without unacceptable degradation of stiffness, strength, or aerodynamic profile.

**DS-REQ-002**  
Healing shall be effective against fatigue crack initiation and limited propagation under representative centrifugal and flapping loads.

**DS-REQ-003**  
The system shall include embedded or integrated sensing sufficient to detect the onset of damage that the healing mechanism is intended to address.

**DS-REQ-004**  
Healing activation (thermal, chemical, pressure, or other) shall be compatible with flight and ground operations and shall not introduce new single-point safety hazards.

**DS-REQ-005**  
Mass penalty of the healing network and agents shall be explicitly traded against the safety and maintenance benefit.

**DS-REQ-006**  
Environmental resistance (moisture, temperature extremes, erosion, fluids) of the healed state shall meet or exceed the baseline blade material requirements.

---

## Initial Coupon & Subscale Test Matrix (High-Level)

1. **Baseline coupons** — conventional composite or hybrid blade material for comparison.
2. **Healing-agent coupons** — microcapsule, microvascular, or thermoplastic systems under evaluation.
3. **Cyclic fatigue testing** — representative R-ratio and load levels for spar and skin elements.
4. **Damage introduction + healing cycle** — controlled crack or impact, then activate healing, then re-test residual strength/stiffness.
5. **Environmental exposure** — humidity, temperature cycling, fluid exposure before and after healing.
6. **Sensing integration trials** — verify that damage detection works with the healing architecture.

Detailed test parameters, specimen geometries, and acceptance criteria will be developed as proprietary analysis packages.

---

## Nice-to-Have Materials Features

- Multiple healing cycles (not single-shot).
- Autonomous detection + healing without pilot or ground intervention for small damage.
- Self-indicating healed zones (visual or sensor-readable).
- Compatibility with existing blade manufacturing and repair processes where practical.
- Extension of the same technology family to critical fuselage and attachment fittings.
